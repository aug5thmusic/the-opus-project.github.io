# Source check: The Favorite Vienna Waltz

Checked 7 October 2026. The complete anonymous piano work is the five printed systems on PDF page 1 of Library of Congress item [2014568419](https://www.loc.gov/item/2014568419/), resource `music.musm1a1-10161`. The verso is not music. Publisher: G. Willig's Musical Magazine, No. 171 Chesnut St., Philadelphia. The library gives the date as **before 1820?**; the question mark is retained. No composer, editor, arranger, or plate number is supplied on the printed leaf. Do not infer Schubert from this generic title.

The sole musical source for independent transcriptions is the printed [direct PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/music/musm1a1-10161/musm1a1-10161.pdf#page=1). Download SHA-256: `e9f9985beaaf9c3c0c5ea9d51b6c81f5df85eb358f57bb88ae3b24dba6016225`. The library's [IIIF image view](https://www.loc.gov/resource/music.musm1a1-10161/?sp=1) provides higher resolution of the same leaf, not a second musical edition.

The source is engraved, complete, and legible. Scope includes all sections, both hands, the change from two to three flats, ornaments, two pairs of numbered endings, Fine, and D.C. Later pencil annotations (crosses, numerical annotations, short strokes) are distinguished from the printed score and excluded. Five source systems contain 34 written bar units when each first and second ending is counted separately, plus the two-sixteenth pickup into the final section. Some ending bars are deliberately short to accommodate that pickup.

## Eligibility

The edition was published in the United States before 1820, far earlier than the U.S. public-domain publication cutoff. Its unknown authorship is retained; no modern editorial edition is used. This record documents eligibility for U.S. publication of a new CC0 encoding; the linked scan remains governed by its own library record. The music and source edition are over two centuries old. The library rights paragraph is general collection language, not evidence of a modern copyright claim for this leaf.

## Existing-encoding scout

Web searches on the date above used the exact title `"favorite Vienna waltz"`, variant `"favourite Vienna waltz"`, and publisher-qualified `"Vienna Waltz" "Willig"`, with transcription, LilyPond, MusicXML, MIDI, ABC, and MuseScore terms. Separate indexed searches restricted the exact title to Mutopia, OpenScore, MuseScore.com, and GitHub. No matching existing digital notation encoding or modern engraving of this specific complete Willig piano edition was found.

Results included the LOC scan, historical library inventories, and the [UMKC sheet-music inventory](https://libweb.umkc.edu/Manuscripts/Collections/sheet-music-finding-aid.pdf), which lists a Willig Vienna Waltz as a historical holding. A thesis about Czerny's variations on a favourite Vienna waltz refers to Schubert's differently identified theme and does not establish attribution of this anonymous leaf. Generic Vienna Waltz results and different works are not matches.

This is a bounded **no matching encoding found** finding, not a universal claim that nobody has ever transcribed the tune. Unindexed, private, differently titled, or unpublished transcriptions may exist. No notes were obtained from another encoding. Two alternative candidates were rejected before selection: Farrenc Op.50 No.3 has a commercial modern engraving; La Pantalon Waltz has a Village Music Project ABC transcription.

## Source findings

No unresolved printed pitch or duration has been identified in the selected work. Ending lengths, printed repeat signs, and return directions are preserved. The score uses modern engraving of beam/slur/volta placement without preserving the sheet's ornamental title typography or pencil additions. Numbered source endings become conventional LilyPond volta brackets.
